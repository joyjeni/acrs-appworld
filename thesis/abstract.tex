\chapter*{Abstract}

Language-model agents now act inside applications on a person's behalf: they find the right APIs, call
them, read the results and change the state of the world. Over many steps their reliability compounds
downward, and the systems that perform best on public agent benchmarks rely on frontier models and
report only task success, not cost, collateral damage or robustness to manipulated tool output. In the
literature the four operations every step needs (retrieving APIs, choosing a model, deciding whether an
action can be trusted, and managing a growing history) are optimised separately and per step.

This thesis proposes the Adaptive Context Reasoning System (ACRS), a closed-loop architecture in which
four mechanisms share one episode state. \emph{Session-Adaptive Tool Scoring} (SATR) ranks APIs by their
utility given what the episode has already used, with training-free signals. \emph{Risk-Weighted Policy
Routing} (APRR) is a contextual-bandit router between model tiers whose exploration changes sign with
the episode's risk. \emph{Byzantine-Tolerant Consensus} (MNCD) certifies an answer only when independent
agents agree on grounded evidence, and certifies a state-changing action only when independently
generated candidate programs, executed from a saved state, agree on the change they cause; it abstains
otherwise, and its threshold is set by Learn-then-Test so that the error rate of executed actions carries
a finite-sample guarantee. \emph{PID-Controlled Provenance Pruning} (FCNP) compresses history under a
budget set by a proportional--integral controller while holding a fidelity floor. A deny-by-default
policy-as-code stage decides which certified actions may execute without a person, and every decision
is logged in a tamper-evident chain.

ACRS is evaluated in three layers. On 100 sessionised ToolBench sessions with simulated agents and a
leak-free protocol (94 of the 100 test gold sets were found in the training data and removed), SATR
improves held-out NDCG@5 by 12.4\,\% over BM25, MNCD lowers accepted errors from 24.0\,\% to 0.6\,\%,
FCNP meets its fidelity floor on 99.7\,\% of turns while removing 47\,\% of tokens, and with 15\,\% of
endpoints poisoned the governance stage cuts the success of injection attacks from 29.3\,\% to 5.7\,\%.
On KCC-Sessions, built from real Kisan Call Centre farmer queries, the integrated loop lowers executed
errors from 11.3\,\% to 0.51\,\% and injection success from 27.8\,\% to 0.86\,\%, while routing pesticide-dose
and entitlement questions to a human advisor. On AppWorld, ACRS is run with open-weight Qwen3 models on a
single free or low-cost GPU and scored with the benchmark's official evaluator, its collateral-damage
tests and paired significance tests (Chapter~\ref{ch:appworld}). Code and aggregate results are public.
